In plain terms

Policy: the decision-maker in training; a language model in the full build, a tiny stand-in here so it trains live.

Environment: creates practice storms and judges answers with physics: storm scale, thrust against drag, simulated survival.

Reward: one point per verified reasoning step, compared within a group of attempts.

Try being the policy (reference spacecraft, first fleet asset's altitude)

    
    
    Why step-by-step checks
    A single score can't say which decision was wrong. Checking each step with physics gives a precise, auditable reasoning trail, which operators and insurers can inspect afterwards.

    In plain terms
    Task: atmosphere models are wrong during storms; learn the correction from altitude, solar flux, storm index and time since onset.
    Quantum part: inputs become qubit rotations, pass two trainable entangling layers, and are measured; a small classical layer gives the correction.
    Honest benchmark: same data, same size, same optimiser. Results vary between runs; neither model is guaranteed to win.
